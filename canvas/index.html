Foreword

My Yeye counts growth in rings. Each morning for a decade, he disappeared into the backyard to tend to our apricot tree, staking its slender trunk with cloth-bound planks. His unhurried movements became the pulse of our garden, a rhythm as constant as his cultivation.

As he worked, Yeye told stories. He planted in me the roots of Chinese mythology: Chang’e’s elixir of immortality, Monkey King’s defiant climb against the heavens. These weren’t simply fables—they were puzzles. Why does she seek eternity? What drives his rebellion? With each new question, Yeye encouraged me to dig deeper, to tug at loose threads and see where they unraveled.

Yeye’s advice guides my chase to understand the quirks of human nature through stories. I dissect the intricacies of West African spider folklore, where cultural ideology is encoded in power dynamics and ancestral tradition; I trace the etymology of Sanskrit mantras, unearthing the entwinement of language and spirituality. My process always begins simply: a slew of ‘why would he?’s and ‘how come they?’s, followed by a patient persistence to peel back each tree ring of what drives and defines us.

Nowadays, Yeye doesn’t always feel well to leave his bed, but the apricot tree no longer needs his care—its roots can venture deep to find its own sustenance. As the apricots and I continue upwards in his absence, I carry the lessons he sowed: Monkey King’s rebellion, Chang’e’s longing, and the joy of understanding humanity—one deliberate, unhurried question at a time.
